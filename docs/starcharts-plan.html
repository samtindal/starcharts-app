01 The product

Starcharts competes on delight and legibility, not depth. The positioning, decided, is the casual astrology consumer: every feature filters through "would someone with casual interest get it in five seconds?" Depth tooling like house systems stays deferred.

The hook

The wheel shows current planetary positions. Grab any planet and drag it around the zodiac and the date changes to match: drag the Moon for hours and days, drag Saturn for years, drag Pluto for centuries. Aspect lines (conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition) appear and dissolve as you scrub.

Why it can win

The top tier of web astrology renders static images from form posts. A single canonical timestamp driving a 60fps draggable wheel is a categorically different experience, and it is shareable in a way form results are not. Starcharts can out-play incumbents and be first through the MCP door with a consumer brand attached.

Three revenue surfaces

Ads (primary). Programmatic SEO across every aspect pair, sign, and a daily sky page builds thousands of long-tail pages that each carry ads. The draggable toy drives session depth, which is what makes the inventory valuable. Ads sit on content pages and below the wheel, never over the interaction.

Paid reports (secondary). One-time PDFs (natal, year ahead, relationship blueprint) at ~$9.99, free web previews, generated entirely by interpret.ts and ranked by aspectStrength. Billed via a Merchant of Record (tax and PCI handled), global from day one, no account needed. Hedges Google-ranking risk. Accounts, subscriptions, and a paid app stay deferred.

MCP server (tertiary). The first consumer-branded astrology MCP. A free tier returns positions with attribution and a link back, so every AI answer becomes a referral. A paid tier covers natal, transits, synastry, and batch queries. Distribution first, revenue second.

Definition of done, v1 launch

Live clock accurate to under 0.1° vs reference; all 12 points draggable at 30fps+ on mid-tier mobile; 5 aspect types with correct orbs and tap-to-explain; date input working (birthdate/natal inputs are P2, not required for v1); 500+ indexed content pages; PWA installable; MCP live with a free tier and one paid path; ads serving on content pages; Lighthouse mobile 90+.

03 Architecture

A pure TypeScript calculation core is the keystone. Web, MCP, and the OG-image renderer all import the same functions, so they can never disagree on a number. Single source of truth for chart state: one UTC timestamp.

State model

Everything derives from one UTC timestamp: positions, aspects, and the URL. The drag interaction writes the timestamp via an inverse ephemeris solver. No duplicated derived state.

The hard problem

Forward is easy (date to longitudes). Dragging inverts it: given a planet at a longitude, find the date. It is non-unique near retrograde loops, so the solver searches nearest-in-time and prefers continuity with the drag direction.

Rendering

Hand-built SVG, not a chart library, layered as zodiac ring, ticks, aspect lines, glyphs. It scales crisply, animates via CSS, and doubles as the server-side OG-image renderer.

Nautical theme tokens

bg navy #0A1628 gold, signs #C9A227 silver, planets #AEB9C4 verdigris, soft aspects #6FA08B steel blue, hard aspects #6E9BC5 parchment #E8DCC4

Signs gold, planets and nodes silver, so the two layers read instantly. Never red, pink, purple, or orange and yellow. Astrological glyphs carry U+FE0E to force monochrome text rendering rather than emoji.
